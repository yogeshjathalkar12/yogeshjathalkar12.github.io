VAHAK-AI

VAHAK-AI

A retrofit kit and software stack that lets the Indian Army’s existing heavy trucks drive themselves over rough terrain, keep navigating when satellite signals are jammed, and travel as a coordinated convoy.

Defence logistics Autonomous ground vehicles Retrofit kit Indigenous navigation
The problem 01

Army supply trucks carry heavy loads over high-altitude passes, soft desert sand and flooded jungle tracks. The driver faces the terrain and, on predictable supply routes, the threat of ambush, both at once.

Autonomy built for roads does not carry over. Off-road there are no lane markings, signals or detailed maps, and a loaded truck has to judge whether the ground will hold it before it commits.

The Army does not need a self-driving car. It needs a truck that can look after itself.

What VAHAK-AI is 02

VAHAK-AI adds autonomy to the trucks the Army already operates instead of replacing them. A soldier in the cab can take back control at any moment, and cutting power to the kit returns the truck to fully manual driving.

01

Drive-by-wire retrofit

A bolt-on kit that steers, brakes, accelerates and changes gear, designed to be fitted to existing heavy trucks without rebuilding them.

02

Terrain-aware driving

The truck reads the ground ahead, sorts it into safe, risky and impassable, and plans a path that accounts for its own weight and load.

03

Sovereign navigation

India’s NavIC is the primary position source. If it is jammed or lost, the truck keeps its position from its own motion sensors and cameras.

04

Convoy coordination

One lead truck carries the full sensor set and the trucks behind follow its path over an encrypted link, which keeps the cost of equipping a convoy down.

05

Command dashboard

A fleet view for the forward operations team showing where each truck is, how it is doing and which vehicle is likely to need maintenance before it fails.

Where it stands 03

VAHAK-AI has been proposed against the Indian Army’s autonomous load carrier trucks challenge. Here is what exists today and what does not.

Programme Proposal submitted in May 2026 to iDEX ADITI 4.0, problem statement 8: Autonomous Load Carrier Trucks. Submitted
Software The vehicle software and the command dashboard are written and pass their automated tests, including the safety rules, navigation filter, path planner and encrypted convoy messaging. Built
On a vehicle Not yet. The software has not run on a truck, and the retrofit hardware has not been built or fitted. Not started
Terrain model Needs training on Indian terrain imagery before the truck can classify ground. Autonomy stays off without it. Pending data
