Human Review & Continuous Learning

The model proposes, a person decides. Trusted moderators check every detection, fix the boxes it got wrong, and reject photos that should not be used. Their corrections are what the model learns from next.

Ask about moderating All features
The problem

A model that trains on its own guesses only gets more confidently wrong.Alaka keeps a human between the model and its own training data, and lets the model learn most from exactly the cases it got wrong.

How it works

Step by step.

01

The AI proposes boxes

Each new report appears in a moderation queue with the model's boxes drawn over the photo.

02

A moderator reviews it

Approve the boxes as they are, drag and resize them, change the class, draw a missing one, or reject the photo entirely.

03

Reviewed photos are collected

Approved and corrected photos become training examples. A photo confirmed to contain nothing is kept as a useful negative example.

04

The model is retrained in batches

Once enough reviewed photos have built up, a fine-tuning run starts on cloud GPUs. The new model only replaces the live one if it scores at least as well on photos it has not trained on.

What you get

The details.

A purpose-built reviewer

A web reviewer with drag, resize, redraw and class-change tools. It works from any browser.

Several trusted moderators

Access is granted per person and every decision records who made it.

Nothing goes live blindly

New model versions are scored against held-out photos before they replace the old one, and every run is logged.

Wrong boxes become the lesson

When a moderator corrects a box, the correction is the training signal.

Reviewing is live. Automated retraining is being switched on; until the first run completes, the live model is the one Alaka launched with.
